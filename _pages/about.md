I am a computational biologist at the University of Connecticut Health Center. My lab builds statistical and machine learning models of cells and tissues, with a focus on how the stroma shapes invasion in maternal-fetal disease and in cancer.

My current work develops prognostic models for maternal-fetal disorders such as pre-eclampsia and fetal growth disorders, using stromal transcriptomics. I use semi-supervised learning on large single-cell RNAseq datasets, and graph neural networks over spatial single-cell transcriptomics from patient samples, to relate fibroblast transcriptional states to systemic disease states. This work is supported by my NICHD R00 award.

Before this I studied the evolved resistance of stroma to invasion, and its consequences for placental disorders such as placenta accreta and preeclampsia, as well as for cancer. I also studied how oscillatory hypoxia changes gene regulation in cancer and fibroblast cells. My earlier work includes graph-theoretic prediction of protein and genetic interactions, mathematical models of paracrine communication between cells, and signal processing for implantable neural electrode arrays.

I received my PhD in Biomedical Engineering from Johns Hopkins University (2017), my MS in Electrical Engineering from Michigan State University (2005), and my B.Tech in Electrical Engineering from IIT Delhi (2003).

**Interests:** computational modeling at cellular and tissue levels; AI over interaction networks for prognostics and -omics data; statistical models for heterotypic datasets.
